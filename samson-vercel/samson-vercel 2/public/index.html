4 seasonal visits

Every season brings new pests.

Spring
Visit 01 of 04

Spring. Everything wakes up.

Ant colonies get busy, termites swarm and queen wasps start new nests. Blacklegged tick nymphs become active as the weather warms.

AntsTermitesBees & WaspsTicks
Summer
Visit 02 of 04

Summer. Peak season outside.

Mosquitoes breed in standing water, wasp colonies grow and tick nymphs stay active into early summer, right when families spend the most time outdoors.

MosquitoesBees & WaspsTicksFleas
Fall
Visit 03 of 04

Fall. Everyone wants in.

Stink bugs, lady beetles and boxelder bugs gather on sunny walls looking for a place to spend the winter, and mice follow the warmth indoors. Adult ticks are active too.

Stink BugsLady BeetlesBoxelder BugsMice
Winter
Visit 04 of 04

Winter. The quiet season is not so quiet.

Mice and rats nest in walls and attics. Overwintering pests wander out on warm days, and adult blacklegged ticks can be active whenever it is above freezing.

MiceRatsStink BugsTicks

Why ticks matter here

Northeast Pennsylvania is tick country.

In 2023, counties in the northeast area of Pennsylvania reported the highest rates of Lyme disease in the state.

Pennsylvania Department of Health, 2023 Lyme and Other Tickborne Diseases Surveillance Report
  • 67 of 67Pennsylvania counties

    Blacklegged ticks carrying the Lyme disease bacteria have been found in all 67 Pennsylvania counties, according to the state Department of Health.

    Pennsylvania Department of Health
  • #2in the nation

    Pennsylvania reported the second highest number of Lyme disease cases of any state in 2023.

    Pennsylvania Department of Health
  • 25+tick species

    At least 25 tick species occur in Pennsylvania. The blacklegged tick has been expanding its range and can now be found throughout the state.

    Penn State Extension
  • 1,292anaplasmosis cases

    Lyme is not the only risk. Pennsylvania reported 1,292 cases of anaplasmosis in 2023, another disease spread by the blacklegged tick.

    Pennsylvania Department of Health

Make your yard less welcoming to ticks

  • Keep grass mowed and clear away leaf litter.
  • Place a 3 foot wide barrier of wood chips or gravel between your lawn and wooded areas.
  • Stack firewood neatly in a dry, sunny spot.
  • Check yourself, your kids and your pets after time outdoors.
  • Ask your veterinarian about tick prevention for your pets.

Yard tips from the CDC. Pair them with our flea and tick treatments.
