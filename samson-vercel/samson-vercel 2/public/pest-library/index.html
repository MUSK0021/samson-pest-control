Pest library

Know your pests.

The most common household and outdoor pests in Northeast Pennsylvania: what they look like, the signs they leave behind and how to keep them out.

Showing all 18 pests

Carpenter ants, pavement ants, odorous house ants

Carpenter ants are large black ants that hollow out moist or damaged wood to nest. They do not eat wood, but a mature colony can weaken it over time. Smaller ants like pavement ants and odorous house ants trail in looking for food and water.

Signs to look for

  • Trails of ants along counters, baseboards or foundations
  • Piles of sawdust like frass below wood trim
  • Winged ants swarming indoors in spring

Prevention tips

  • Wipe up crumbs and sticky spills quickly
  • Fix leaks and replace water damaged wood
  • Trim branches that touch the house

Yellowjackets, paper wasps, bald-faced hornets

Queen wasps start new nests in spring, and colonies grow all summer. Yellowjackets become especially defensive in late summer and fall. Honey bees are important pollinators, so a honey bee swarm is best relocated by a local beekeeper.

Signs to look for

  • Papery nests under eaves, decks or railings
  • Steady traffic in and out of a hole in the ground or siding
  • Wasps around trash cans and outdoor meals

Prevention tips

  • Keep lids on trash and recycling
  • Check eaves and railings for small nests in spring
  • Never seal a nest opening in a wall. The wasps may chew inside

German cockroach (Blattella germanica)

German cockroaches are the most common indoor species. They hitchhike in on grocery bags, boxes and used appliances, then hide in warm, tight spaces near food and water. A small problem can become a large one quickly.

Signs to look for

  • Roaches running when you turn on a light
  • Pepper like droppings in cabinets and drawers
  • A musty odor in kitchens and bathrooms

Prevention tips

  • Inspect boxes and used appliances before bringing them inside
  • Store food in sealed containers
  • Fix dripping faucets and leaks

House flies, cluster flies, fruit flies

House flies breed in garbage and manure and can carry germs to food. Cluster flies are common in rural homes: they gather on sunny walls in fall, slip into attics and wall voids for winter, and wander out on warm days.

Signs to look for

  • Flies gathering on sunny windows
  • Sluggish flies indoors on warm winter days
  • Tiny flies hovering around fruit or drains

Prevention tips

  • Keep window and door screens in good repair
  • Take out trash often and keep bins clean
  • Seal gaps around windows, siding and soffits before fall

Cat flea (Ctenocephalides felis)

The cat flea is the flea most often found on both cats and dogs. Eggs fall off pets into carpets, furniture and yards, and flea pupae can wait weeks before hatching. That is why pets, home and yard should be treated together.

Signs to look for

  • Pets scratching, licking or chewing their fur
  • Small itchy bites around ankles
  • Black specks of flea dirt in pet bedding

Prevention tips

  • Ask your veterinarian about flea prevention for every pet
  • Wash pet bedding in hot water
  • Vacuum often and empty the vacuum outside

House mosquitoes and other species

Mosquitoes lay eggs in standing water, and even small containers like buckets, tires and clogged gutters can produce biting adults. West Nile virus has been detected in Pennsylvania mosquitoes, and the state runs a West Nile virus surveillance program.

Signs to look for

  • Bites after dusk or in shady areas
  • Standing water in buckets, tires or clogged gutters
  • Wigglers (larvae) in birdbaths and containers

Prevention tips

  • Dump standing water every week
  • Clean gutters so they drain
  • Change birdbath water often

House spiders, wolf spiders, cellar spiders

Most spiders in our area are harmless to people and eat other insects. A home with many spiders usually has plenty of prey, so controlling other pests is part of controlling spiders.

Signs to look for

  • Webs in corners, basements and garages
  • Egg sacs tucked into quiet spaces
  • Large wolf spiders running across floors in fall

Prevention tips

  • Knock down webs and egg sacs regularly
  • Reduce clutter in basements and garages
  • Use yellow bug lights outdoors to attract fewer insects

Blacklegged (deer) tick (Ixodes scapularis), American dog tick

Blacklegged tick nymphs are most active in late spring and early summer and are so small they are very hard to spot. Adults are active in fall and on winter days above freezing. The Pennsylvania Department of Health reports that most Lyme disease cases are linked to nymphs.

Signs to look for

  • Ticks on pets after walks or yard time
  • Tall grass, leaf litter and brush along wood lines
  • Deer and mice moving through your yard

Prevention tips

  • Keep grass mowed and clear leaf litter
  • Put a 3 foot wood chip or gravel barrier between lawn and woods
  • Do tick checks on family and pets after time outdoors

Multicolored Asian lady beetle (Harmonia axyridis)

Multicolored Asian lady beetles eat aphids outdoors, but in fall they look for protected places to spend the winter, often inside homes. They do not breed indoors, but they can stain surfaces and give off an unpleasant odor.

Signs to look for

  • Beetles gathering on sunny south and west walls in fall
  • Clusters in window frames and attics
  • Yellow stains and a bitter odor when disturbed

Prevention tips

  • Seal cracks around windows, doors and siding before fall
  • Vacuum beetles instead of crushing them
  • Repair torn screens and attic vents

Boxelder bug (Boisea trivittata)

Boxelder bugs feed mainly on the seeds of boxelder and maple trees. They do not bite or damage the home, but large numbers gather on buildings in fall and slip into wall voids, and their droppings can stain.

Signs to look for

  • Groups of black and red bugs on sunny walls
  • Bugs around boxelder or maple trees
  • Bugs appearing indoors on warm winter days

Prevention tips

  • Seal gaps around siding, windows and utility lines
  • Rake up seeds under boxelder trees
  • Vacuum bugs that make it inside

Brown marmorated stink bug (Halyomorpha halys)

The brown marmorated stink bug arrived from Asia and was first identified in Allentown, Pennsylvania. Each fall they look for a warm place to overwinter, and homes are a favorite. They are harmless to people but release a pungent smell.

Signs to look for

  • Shield shaped brown bugs on siding and screens
  • Bugs crawling out of curtains and light fixtures in winter
  • A strong odor when one is crushed

Prevention tips

  • Seal cracks and gaps before temperatures drop
  • Repair window screens and door sweeps
  • Vacuum them up rather than crushing them

House centipede (Scutigera coleoptrata)

The house centipede has 15 pairs of long legs and prefers damp places like basements and bathrooms. It hunts other insects and spiders, so an active centipede population often means there is plenty of prey and moisture.

Signs to look for

  • Long legged centipedes darting across basement floors
  • Sightings in bathrooms, bathtubs and damp rooms
  • Other insects they hunt

Prevention tips

  • Run a dehumidifier in damp basements
  • Fix leaky pipes and improve drainage
  • Clear clutter and boxes off basement floors

Field crickets, house crickets, camel crickets

Field crickets move toward buildings as nights cool in late summer and fall. Camel crickets do not chirp, but they gather in cool, damp basements, crawl spaces and garages, and jump when startled.

Signs to look for

  • Chirping at night indoors
  • Hump backed camel crickets jumping in basements and garages
  • Chewed fabric or paper in storage areas

Prevention tips

  • Reduce moisture in basements and crawl spaces
  • Install door sweeps and seal foundation gaps
  • Keep grass and plants trimmed back from the foundation

European earwig (Forficula auricularia)

Earwigs hide by day in damp, dark places like mulch, stones and planters, and come out at night to feed. Despite the old myth, they do not crawl into ears, and their pincers are mostly for show.

Signs to look for

  • Insects with rear pincers under mats and planters
  • Chewed leaves and flowers in gardens
  • Earwigs indoors after hot, dry spells

Prevention tips

  • Pull mulch a few inches back from the foundation
  • Fix drainage near downspouts
  • Move firewood and stones away from the house

Garden millipedes

Millipedes have two pairs of legs on most body segments and feed on decaying leaves and mulch. They sometimes migrate in large numbers, but indoors they dry out and die quickly because they need moisture to survive.

Signs to look for

  • Brown or black many legged worms curled up on floors
  • Numbers of them on patios and foundations after rain
  • Dead millipedes in basements and garages

Prevention tips

  • Keep mulch and leaf litter away from the foundation
  • Seal gaps under doors and around the foundation
  • Fix moisture problems in basements

Silverfish (Lepisma saccharinum)

Silverfish have a tapered body and three long tail like appendages. They prefer humid spaces and feed on starches found in paper, glue, fabric and dry foods.

Signs to look for

  • Silvery, carrot shaped insects in bathrooms and basements
  • Small holes or yellow stains on paper and fabric
  • Damage to books, photos and wallpaper paste

Prevention tips

  • Lower humidity with fans or a dehumidifier
  • Store papers and photos in sealed plastic bins
  • Keep dry foods in airtight containers

House mice, deer mice, white-footed mice

Mice move indoors as the weather cools and can squeeze through an opening about the size of a dime. They contaminate food and surfaces with droppings and urine, and deer mice can carry hantavirus.

Signs to look for

  • Droppings in drawers, cabinets and along walls
  • Gnaw marks on food packages and wood
  • Scratching sounds in walls and ceilings at night

Prevention tips

  • Seal gaps around pipes, vents and utility lines
  • Store food, birdseed and pet food in sealed containers
  • Keep firewood and brush away from the house

Help from Samson: Residential Pest Control

Norway rat (Rattus norvegicus)

Norway rats dig burrows in soil near buildings and travel the same paths each night. They gnaw constantly, including on wiring, and can squeeze through a gap about the size of a quarter.

Signs to look for

  • Burrows along foundations, sheds and woodpiles
  • Large droppings and greasy rub marks along walls
  • Gnawed wires, pipes or feed bags

Prevention tips

  • Store animal feed in metal containers with lids
  • Remove clutter, brush and debris near buildings
  • Seal holes around foundations, pipes and utility lines
